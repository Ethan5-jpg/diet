# V12 methods, targets and limits

Status: locally reviewed against completed V6–V11 evidence on 2026-11-09; native V12 execution pending. Review context/source hashes are in `review_context.json` and `POST_V11_REVIEW.md`. Public methods sources were checked on 2026-11-09 using generic statistical queries; no unpublished project data were sent to external services.

## Question and analysis unit

For the established discovery population, does the conditional association between person-average fiber density and person-level D_Glu differ by oral/gut microbial features? Fiber density is g/1,000 kcal; D_Glu retains the existing endpoint definition and mg/dL units. This is not a meal-level or within-person response analysis. The two household five-fold partitions contain the same people. Three household inner folds select prediction penalties inside each outer training role.

The ordinary minimum model is `Y = background + diet + microbes + diet*microbes + error`. A microbial main effect can improve outcome prediction without changing a dietary association. Statistical modification is scale- and contrast-specific; no causal mechanism follows merely from a product coefficient.

The historical summary counts 307 within-batch workflows, including repeated algorithms/representations; this is not 307 independent algorithms or confirmations. Only displayed screenshot values are known locally. V11's fusion improvement and zero/negative explicit corrections are preserved as development evidence, not repurposed as positive modification findings or an exclusion of all biological heterogeneity.

### Timing and window boundaries

The source calendar audit reports oral sample-date-minus-CGM-end P10/P50/P90 of 24/68/112.80 days and gut values of 5/10/21 days. The exact collection-time meaning/provenance of those calendar fields was not independently revalidated. This does not establish causal ordering and must not be used to declare a pre-exposure modifier or to create a timing proxy for prediction. The timestamp/semantics of D_Glu must retain their original endpoint definition; they are not inferred simply from the CGM envelope, and this batch does not redefine D_Glu as a meal response.

The established nutrient context uses per-person valid-day means. The corrected 37,676 joint macro days (3,330 people with >=3) only require the three macros to coexist on a food-record day. They are not proof of exact CGM-day alignment. A prepared diet/CGM common-day table covers 3,025 people and 21,262 days under prior restrictions; absence from that table does not prove no common day. Neither table is used to exclude participants or change this person-level target. The corrected lipid >=3-day count is 3,333, not 3,332; this was a transcription correction, not a server-data change.

## Primary target: fixed ecological coordinates

For each person, close nonnegative raw microbial profiles to relative abundances. Shannon entropy is `-sum(p log p)` with natural logarithms; dominance is `max(p)`. The ordered marker vector is oral entropy, oral dominance, gut entropy, gut dominance. Targets are `T_j = ((fiber_density - 10)/5) * M_j`. No taxon selection, outcome screen, data-driven subgroup split or fold-specific target rotation is used.

These ecological markers are convenient summaries of composition, not validated mechanisms. Alpha diversity/dominance can depend on sampling, processing and measurement. Correlated ecological coordinates can be difficult to separate; report their covariance and failures of target rank/conditioning. A marker's coordinate IQR contrast holds other working-model terms equal; it is not an intervention on entropy or a claim that arbitrary microbial combinations can occur.

## Matched additive/background basis

For every training role, reuse V4 training-fitted CLR preprocessing, fiber spline and phenotype outlier handling. Reuse the established host design (age, sex, energy, smoking, fiber linear/RCS and existing fiber×age/sex terms). Add V10 full macronutrient/sodium/lifestyle encoding with training medians, missingness and alcohol categories, and the existing separate UPF bounds. No midpoint, daily outcome, time-lag proxy or new exclusion is invented.

Both microbial sites occur in every null and augmented model. Per site retain 30 outcome-blind training PCA coordinates where dimension permits, plus training-quantile univariate RCS curvature for the first six. Also include log richness, entropy, Simpson diversity, dominance and evenness with univariate main-effect curvature. Nonmicrobial background receives additional univariate curvature and fiber-linear/RCS×background terms. These background interactions are retained in both comparison arms to reduce attribution of measured background modification to microbes. This does not guarantee all confounding or model approximation error is removed.

Training-only centering/scaling is used for numerical prediction designs. Every diet-dependent host/background column is recomputed when evaluating a fixed fiber contrast. The same PCA operation is used for training and validation. No outer outcome is used for preprocessing, PC selection, nuisance basis definition or penalty selection. All transformations, selected coefficients and fold-specific taxon loadings are retained on the server.

This is a matched and relatively rich working basis, not a proof that nuisance modeling is sufficient. Its absolute OOF null R² and effective rank are reported. V11's stronger fusion can contain implicit interactions and therefore cannot be substituted as an additive null or subtracted to quantify a pure interaction increment. Any residual misspecification remains a possible explanation of a detected projection coefficient.

## Fold-stratified finite-basis partial regression

Feature maps and their complexity are fixed using the outer training households, without outcome-based selection for this primary inference. Once maps are fixed, fit the inference working model `Y_i = B_f(i) beta_f + T_i theta + error_i`, with a separate nuisance coefficient vector for each held-out fold and four common ecological interaction coefficients. Use rank-revealing QR within each held-out fold to partial (a) frozen-preprocessed Y, (b) raw Y and (c) the four fixed target columns with respect to that fold's main-effect basis. Pool these residuals and solve `theta = (T*' T*)^-1 T*' y*`.

This is ordinary Frisch–Waugh–Lovell partial regression for a model with block-diagonal fold-specific nuisance bases. The held-out outcomes are used to estimate statistical regression coefficients in this inference branch. They never train, tune or select the separate OOF prediction models. This separation is essential: fitted inference residuals are not advertised as out-of-sample outcome predictions. This implementation is not a generic DML or causal estimator.

Interpretation is conditional on the finite working-model specification and learned feature maps. An omitted nonlinear main effect or background modifier can create a nonzero interaction projection without true biological modification. Frozen outcome preprocessing uses training-derived thresholds; raw-outcome sensitivity helps expose sensitivity to that choice, but does not remove historical discovery selection.

For variance, retain each nuisance QR basis Q_f. The full model hat matrix is the block-diagonal nuisance projection plus the residual-target projection. Within household g, adjust the full regression residual by `(I - H_gg)^(-1/2)` under a working identity covariance, then form scores `T*_g' adjusted_residual_g`. This CR2-style leverage adjustment includes the nuisance leverage, which matters when the background basis is substantial. Generalized inverse handling is allowed only for zero-information nuisance-saturated directions; otherwise the test becomes UNAVAILABLE. Apply the additional G/(G-1) factor to the score sandwich.

Rademacher household score multipliers generate centered coefficient perturbations; 1,999 native draws produce joint, site and coordinate reference distributions. This is an approximate influence-score multiplier procedure, not a full restricted-model bootstrap-t refit. Intervals use the household t reference. No Satterthwaite small-sample exactness is claimed. Too few households, deficient target rank or deficient household covariance yields UNAVAILABLE, not a zero P value.

The post-V11 review adds an outcome-blind information-concentration diagnostic. For residual-target design X, let `w = X (X'X)^-1` and `a_gj = sum_(i in g) w_ij^2`; report `G_eff,j = (sum_g a_gj)^2 / sum_g a_gj^2`. Its value is invariant to a coordinate's units. It is a fixed-design diagnostic, not a replacement residual df, Satterthwaite approximation, validated sample-size rule or power estimate. If any of the four fixed coordinates has fewer than 30 effective households, the primary family is UNAVAILABLE instead of selectively dropping that coordinate. No participants are removed from prediction evaluation. Full model maximum household leverage is also retained.

Before native outcomes were accessed, the initial multivariate multiplier Wald test showed mild over-rejection in a fixed nonlinear-null simulation (7.3% at nominal 5% in 1,000 replications). That prototype calibration is retained in the verification record. The multivariate Wald/multiplier P values are therefore **diagnostic only**. The final primary omnibus uses a conservative union test: for coordinate j take `p_j = max(p_t/F,j, p_score-multiplier,j)` and set `p_global = min(1, 4*min_j p_j)`. This tests the global null that all four coefficients are zero, trading multivariate power for a transparent multiplicity bound. The bound relies on adequate individual P-value approximations; simulation remains a check, not a proof of universal validity. Its choice occurred before any V12 native-data analysis, not after viewing a favorable empirical result.

The sole primary test is the four-coordinate conservative-union omnibus in repeat 0. Report repeat 1 as splitting sensitivity. Each site uses its corresponding two-coordinate Bonferroni union; site follow-ups then use Holm across two sites. Coordinate follow-ups use Holm across four conservative coordinate P values, interpreted after the primary omnibus. These adjustments do not correct the many earlier discovery experiments. Raw-Y inference on the identical people is a prespecified sensitivity, not another primary opportunity. Report any discordance with the frozen-preprocessed outcome.

The term primary sets priority within this exploratory batch; it does not retrospectively make the previously reused discovery cohort an untouched confirmatory sample.

For the 10→15 fiber comparison, a coordinate coefficient times the mean of the five training-role IQRs gives the corresponding Q75-minus-Q25 interaction difference in mg/dL. Both pointwise 95% t intervals and four-coordinate Bonferroni 95% family intervals are retained; the updated primary screen displays the family intervals. They condition on reference IQRs, fixed feature maps/outcome-preprocessing thresholds and the working-model specification. They are not full population-quantile uncertainty intervals or measured personal responses. The paired pointwise intervals and conservative max-P primary tests need not yield identical threshold decisions; the declared primary rule controls interpretation.

## Secondary nested prediction comparisons

Registry: ecological markers or the first six composition PCs per site; linear or linear+RCS fiber interaction; oral, gut or both modifier sources. This gives twelve fixed augmented workflows and one common null. All retain both sites' main effects and the same background terms.

Within each training role, center modifier coordinates at their training means before forming diet products. Project each candidate product basis onto the complete matched main design, subtract that projection, remove numerically empty residual columns and training-standardize the residual interaction basis. Apply that same frozen projection when transforming validation rows and fixed dietary contrasts. This outcome-blind orthogonalization prevents product columns from merely duplicating a penalized main-effect direction and thereby weakening its effective shrinkage. No validation row is used to estimate that projection.

Inner household OOF MSE first selects a common main-effect ridge penalty from `[0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1]` at mean-loss scale. With that penalty fixed, each augmented workflow selects an exact no-interaction option or interaction penalty `[0.003, 0.03, 0.3, 3]` using the same inner predictions. Positive options use the residualized interaction basis. Its training orthogonality keeps the main-design coefficients anchored to the matched null in this parameterization; equivalent coefficients in the unprojected product parameterization include the subtracted main projection. The zero option exactly reuses the selected null. Complete outer households remain untouched until choices are fixed.

Report all total pooled OOF R² values and matched ΔR² values, including negatives. Household paired bootstrap (999 native draws) resamples fixed OOF outcomes and predictions within each repeat. It preserves family membership and the pairing of null/augmented models but does not refit learned models or include historical search uncertainty. It supplies no standalone proof of modification.

## Fixed contrasts, support and stability

At fiber densities 10 and 15, recompute all diet terms while holding the represented non-diet inputs fixed. Keep three quantities distinct: total augmented contrast, the centered raw-product block contrast (with residual-basis scales), and the difference between augmented and null total contrasts. The last quantity includes subtraction of the main-design projection; it differs from the raw-product contrast even when the coefficients in the orthogonal main parameterization are unchanged. `interaction_component_sd_mg_dl` reports the raw-product component, while `change_from_null_contrast_sd_mg_dl` reports the full residualized correction. These labels must be preserved in downstream figures.

For each validation person, find up to 100 training neighbors using training-standardised non-diet background, first six site PCs and the four ecological markers. Both contrast endpoints must lie inside those neighbors' training fiber Q10–Q90 range for a local-support flag. This is a diagnostic, not a formal estimate of conditional positivity, and it does not establish support for arbitrary independent changes of microbial coordinates. No person is excluded from primary inference or R² evaluation on this basis; low support is reported and limits interpretation.

Cross-repeat Pearson/Spearman correlations of interaction contrasts use aligned participants, with an additional descriptive correlation on the shared support set. No PC coefficient pooling, sign-based taxon matching, independent-replication claim or extra sample count is permitted. Mean-zero interaction predictions, absent active folds and rank failures remain visible.

The revised compact report separately labels `opt` (positive option selected), `nz` (numerically varying raw-product contrast), raw-product SD, full correction SD, matched ΔR² and the primary inferential P value. Stability contains correlations for both raw-product and full-correction contrasts. Numerical SD is never a test statistic establishing modification. Inferring coefficients from the held-out outcomes in the ordinary regression branch remains distinct from predicting those outcomes out of sample.

## What simulation establishes

The preflight checks household isolation, outcome leakage, exact zero options, replay/resume integrity, fixed-contrast decomposition, scope gates and negative-result preservation. The score-core calibration uses fixed additive finite bases and correlated observations, with null, nonlinear-null, known-interaction and deliberately omitted-background cases. It records rejection-rate Monte Carlo intervals and coefficient coverage. These cases can expose errors and assumption sensitivity, but cannot certify native-model adequacy, universal type-I error, biological validity or independence from previous adaptive discovery.

- Knol MJ, VanderWeele TJ (2012). [Recommendations for presenting analyses of effect modification and interaction](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyr218). Supports stating scales, exposure contrasts, conditional effects and interaction uncertainty; its binary-risk worked measures are not copied into this continuous-outcome analysis.

- Bell RM, McCaffrey DF (2002). [Bias reduction in standard errors for linear regression with multi-stage samples](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/pub/12-001-x/2002002/article/9058-eng.pdf). Primary background for leverage-adjusted, bias-reduced linearization. The implementation uses a working identity covariance and reports its approximate multiplier/t reference explicitly.
- Cameron AC, Gelbach JB, Miller DL (2008). [Bootstrap-based improvements for inference with clustered errors](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.3.414). Background for clustered bootstrap inference; the implementation here is explicitly an influence-score multiplier approximation, not their complete bootstrap-t algorithm.
