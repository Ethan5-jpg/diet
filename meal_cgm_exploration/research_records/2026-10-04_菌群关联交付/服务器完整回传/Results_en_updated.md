# Same-visit associations of four selected microbial candidates

Draft based on the complete terminal return from `gut_same_visit_complete_20261004T133519_129603Z`. All 48 specifications are available at six-significant-digit display precision; the original full-precision tables remain on the server. No models were refitted for this draft.

The primary analysis included 3,631 participants contributing 91,144 eligible meals; the overlapping strict subset comprised 3,398 participants and 85,160 meals. Each participant contributed at least five meals across three dates, with medians of 25 meals and 11 dates in both samples. Outcomes were summarized within participants, who received equal weights in the regression analyses.

For the primary outcome, mean postprandial increment, none of the four selected microbial candidates met the within-family Benjamini–Hochberg threshold of q<0.05 in the primary sample. Clostridium phoceensis (T046) and Ruthenibacterium lactatiformans (T096) had positive coefficients of 0.202 and 0.230 mg/dL per original microbial CLR-Z unit, respectively, but their family q values were 0.0913 and 0.0885. In the strict subset, both associations met the original four-test family threshold: T046, 0.243 mg/dL (pointwise 95% CI, 0.038 to 0.448; q=0.0404); T096, 0.251 mg/dL (0.047 to 0.454; q=0.0404). Both also met the supplementary correction across all twelve meal tests (q12=0.0484 and 0.0474). Multiplicity-adjusted support therefore depended on the sample definition. The samples overlap, and their results do not establish independent replication or a difference between sample-specific effects.

Three candidates were associated with pre-meal glucose in the primary sample: GGB6012_SGB8640 (T023), 0.455 mg/dL per original CLR-Z unit (95% CI, 0.164 to 0.746; q=0.00870); T046, −0.389 mg/dL (−0.688 to −0.090; q=0.0285); and T096, −0.696 mg/dL (−0.996 to −0.396; q=4.42×10⁻⁵). The directions and threshold support were retained in the strict subset and under the supplementary twelve-test meal correction in both samples. No candidate met the corresponding multiplicity threshold for peak rise in either sample. Pre-meal glucose remained a secondary outcome and was not equated with fasting glucose.

For the same-connection whole-CGM reference outcomes, all four candidates were associated with mean glucose in both samples, with positive coefficients for T023 and Eubacterium rectale (T068) and negative coefficients for T046 and T096. T068 was also positively associated with CV and SDw in both samples. In the primary sample, its standardized coefficients were 0.0463 for mean glucose (q=0.000807), 0.0667 for CV (q=0.000268) and 0.0931 for SDw (q=1.23×10⁻⁷). However, T068 did not meet the correction threshold for any of the three meal outcomes. Thus, the measured meal phenotypes did not establish an explanation for its whole-CGM associations. Whole-record and meal outcomes may share glucose observations, and their coefficients use different outcome scales and adjustment sets.

The results describe conditional same-visit associations of candidates selected in earlier analyses of the same cohort. They do not establish causal ordering, clinical benefit from changing a taxon, or formal effect differences between glucose phenotypes.
