# HPP Meal and Glucose Study: concise speaker script

30 September 2026. Six slides, approximately five minutes.

## Slide 1 HPP Meal and Glucose Study

This week, we extended the overall CGM analysis to individual meals. We separated premeal glucose, postmeal increments, and the timing of the response. The most useful findings are the predictive value of premeal information and several small dietary associations that depend on the adjustment model. The date separation and microbiome analyses provide supporting evidence with clear limitations.

## Slide 2 Premeal information improves prediction

For the same person, meals matched by food logs still had a median absolute difference of about six milligrams per deciliter in mean net increment. This variation may reflect both actual changes in state and recording or measurement error. We then trained on earlier dates and tested on later dates. Adding premeal glucose and its trend reduced mean absolute error by zero point seven four four milligrams per deciliter, with a similar estimate in the strict sample. This demonstrates added predictive value in the current task. The target was absolute postmeal glucose, and both the people and meal templates were already known. Generalization to new people or foods remains untested.

## Slide 3 Dietary associations are small and conditional

The dietary analyses operate at two levels. Within a person, a ten percentage point increase in a meal’s NOVA4 energy share was associated with a rise of about zero point one seven two milligrams per deciliter in mean net increment. The statistical evidence is clear, but the magnitude is small. A one-unit increase in the original personal rEDIH Z score was associated with a reduction of about zero point four nine after full adjustment. The background model did not provide comparable support, so the interpretation depends on conditioning on meal nutrients. Personal carbohydrate energy share also showed a negative association after full adjustment, with a change in direction across models. Other scores showed no consistent pattern of lower responses. These are specific observational findings, not established intervention effects.

## Slide 4 Response timing provides additional detail

AMED did not show a supported association with the overall mean response. However, a direct model of the response during minutes sixty to one hundred twenty minus the response during the first sixty minutes showed an association of about minus zero point two seven. This evidence comes from testing the contrast directly. Comparing separate significance labels would not establish the difference. Response timing therefore adds descriptive information beyond the overall mean. Interaction analyses for rEDIH, personal NOVA4, and carbohydrate energy share also suggested smaller carbohydrate-related response contrasts, around zero point one to zero point three six milligrams per deciliter. These depend on their reference contrasts. The rEDIH net interaction did not survive correction in the sample with all seven scores, so these remain exploratory findings.

## Slide 5 Date separation and microbiome are supplementary

All twenty-four associations remained after we separated the dates and removed directly shared CGM readings. This addresses overlap, but the models did not adjust for earlier overall glucose. Persistence of a person’s glucose characteristics is still a possible explanation. We have not established unique information from meal responses. In the main microbiome sample, three of four candidates were associated with premeal glucose. None passed the relevant correction for mean net increment or peak rise. Two net associations appeared in the strict sample and should remain sensitivity findings. Most matched microbiome record dates followed CGM monitoring, while actual collection timing is not fully established. These results can remain observational evidence, but they do not establish a new causal or mediation pathway.

## Slide 6 What this week contributes to the study

The contribution is a set of meal-level phenotypes that extend the original overall CGM analysis. The results we can report are the predictive value of premeal information within the tested setting, small dietary associations under specific adjustment conditions, and the AMED difference between early and late responses. Date separation and microbiome findings belong in supporting analyses, including the negative results. The immediate next step is to consolidate the existing full-precision tables and absolute prediction errors, then complete the results write-up.
